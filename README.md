BrightLearn Data Analytics Case Study: Bright Coffee Shop Analysis (2026)

Project Overview

This project was completed as part of the BrightLearn Data Analytics Case Study (2026). The objective of the project is to analyze historical coffee shop sales data and provide actionable business insights to help the CEO improve revenue, product performance, and sales strategies.
The analysis focuses on:

• Identifying the products that generate the most revenue.
• Determining peak sales periods during the day.
• Understanding sales trends across products and time intervals.
• Providing data-driven recommendations to improve business performance.

Business Problem:

Bright Coffee Shop recently appointed a new CEO whose objective is to grow company revenue and improve product performance. As a Junior Data Analyst, I was tasked with transforming raw transactional data into meaningful insights that support strategic decision-making.
The project aims to:

• Calculate total revenue.
• Identify best-selling and underperforming products.
• Analyze sales by product category.
• Determine peak sales time intervals.
• Analyze store performance.
• Provide recommendations for improving sales performance.
• Present findings through dashboards and presentations.

Tools & Technologies Used:

• Databricks: Used for data cleaning and preprocessing.
• SQL: Executed for core data analysis and querying deep insights.
• Microsoft Excel: Utilized for Pivot tables and preliminary data modeling.
• Microsoft PowerPoint: Applied to build and deliver the final presentation for executive leadership.

Key Insights:

1. Product Performance
 
• Top-Selling Products: Gourmet brewed coffee, Brewed chai tea, and Barista Espresso are the primary drivers of revenue.
• Low-Selling Products: Green beans, Green tea, and Clothing merchandise underperform and represent low inventory turnover.

3. Peak Hours

• Sales heavily concentrate in the morning window from 06:00:00 to 11:59:59, making it the most critical time for revenue generation.

3. Store Performance & Location Variation
   
• Lower Manhattan locations boast remarkably high efficiency. Despite a lower footprint (fewer total stores), they generate total revenue numbers that nearly match the larger-scale operations in Astoria and Hell's Kitchen.

Actionable Strategy Recommendations for the CEO

Based on the data-driven insights above, the following strategic actions are recommended to optimize performance and boost revenue:

• Optimize Morning Operations & Labor Allocation: Maximize peak-hour efficiency between 06:00 and 12:00 by scheduling top-performing staff during these hours. Introduce fast-track mobile ordering lines for the highest-selling items (Gourmet brewed coffee, Brewed chai tea, and Barista Espresso) to prevent morning bottlenecks.
• Expand the Lower Manhattan Footprint: Prioritize Lower Manhattan for future store openings. The exceptional revenue-per-store ratio indicates high demand and strong market fit in this sector, making expansion here highly lucrative compared to more saturated areas.
• Revamp Inventory and Menu Offerings: Reduce the inventory footprint of stagnant items like Green beans and Clothing to free up capital. Run promotional bundles pairing low-selling Green tea with high-margin morning pastries, or consider phasing out underperforming merchandise entirely to focus on high-velocity core beverage sales.
